A persistent Claude Project holding my resume and four years of peer evaluations, kept private and used only by me. Every answer I prepared was mapped back to real evidence in it: a specific project, a specific piece of peer feedback. Nothing invented. That same corpus judged fit — comparing each JD against my actual documented track record to decide whether a role was genuinely the right next step, not just one I could land.

## Problem

When I was looking for my next role, I wanted every interview answer backed by something real, and I wanted to judge whether a role was right for me, not just whether I could get it. Generic interview prep produces polished answers that aren't specific to you.

## How it works

1. **Corpus.** A private Claude Project holds my resume and four years of peer evaluations. Only I use it.
2. **Fit check.** Each job description is compared against my documented track record to decide whether the role is a genuine next step. This filtered where I applied.
3. **Answers.** Prepared answers are mapped back to specific projects and specific peer feedback. Nothing invented.
4. **Question research.** Publicly shared candidate experiences for each company were used to predict likely question sets.

## Key decisions

- **One persistent corpus.** Keeping everything in one Project means every answer and every assessment draws on the same evidence, instead of re-explaining my background in each chat.
- **Evidence or nothing.** If an answer couldn't be traced to a real project or real feedback, it didn't go in.
- **Fit before effort.** Judging fit first meant less time preparing for roles that weren't right.
- **Private by design.** Peer evaluations stay in the private Project. None of it is on this site or in the AI twin's index.

## Result

Several of the predicted questions came up in real interviews, and I landed the role.

## Limits

This is context engineering and research synthesis, not an agent build. Its value depends on the quality of the source documents.
