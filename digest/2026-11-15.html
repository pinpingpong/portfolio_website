Issue #19 · 15 Nov 2026 · Weekly AI Digest

Fable 5.2 Ships,
Claude Goes Office,
Anthropic Hits the Road

Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2, unveiled Claude Sheets, Meetings and Data, and launched its IPO roadshow at $70–78 a share, while OpenAI closed $11B at a $1.25T valuation and Mercor doubled to $20B.

Big News
Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2
On 9 November Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model with different safeguards. Fable 5.2 is available to everyone on the API (claude-fable-5-2) and on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure at $8/$40 per million input/output tokens. Mythos 5.2, without the cyber and biology safeguards, is limited to vetted organisations in Anthropic's Cyber and Life Sciences verification programs. Anthropic says output is up to 30% faster than 5.1, adds an optional fast mode (up to 2.5× faster, at double the price), and reports Fable 5.2 beating Opus 5.5 on its own agentic coding and automation benchmarks, for example 69.9% vs 62.3% on FrontierSWE v2. Safety-classifier blocks are down about 55% from Fable 5.1. Fable 5.2 can also act as an Advisor or Orchestrator that hands simpler work to smaller models.

Anthropic takes on Microsoft Office and starts its IPO roadshow
At an event in San Francisco on 10 November, Anthropic unveiled office tools for knowledge workers: Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta, Claude Data (formerly Claude Dashboards) generally available, and Claude Meetings in beta for Team and Enterprise plans, working only with Google Meet for now. Partners including Adobe, Atlassian and HubSpot can run versions of their apps inside Claude. An Anthropic product leader called Sheets "fairly basic" for now. The same day, Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow: 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70–78 each, listing on Nasdaq as "ANT". Reports citing CNBC say pricing is expected on 18 November, though that date isn't confirmed.

OpenAI closes $11B at a $1.25 trillion valuation
TechCrunch, citing Reuters, reported on 6 November that OpenAI has raised $11B at a $1.25 trillion valuation, with SoftBank and MGX among the investors. It's the first part of a round that could reach $20B in the coming weeks. OpenAI originally sought $30B or more at $1.4 trillion and cut its targets as AI stocks sold off, so the final size of this round is a useful read on how much appetite investors still have for frontier labs.

Mercor doubles its valuation to $20B
Mercor, which pays doctors, lawyers, bankers and other experts to train and grade frontier AI models, announced a $750M Series D at a $20B valuation on 10 November. That's double the $10B it reached with its $350M Series C in October 2025. Reported investors include Benchmark, General Catalyst, Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom, and the round included an employee tender. Revenue figures in coverage conflict and Mercor hasn't published its own, so treat run-rate numbers you see quoted with care. Mercor says it will spend on evaluation research and on covering more occupations.

Tools Worth Knowing
Google ADK 2.14.0
Python · open source · 7 Nov
Google's Agent Development Kit moves A2A, execution sandboxes, evaluation and core runtime modules from experimental to stable. New in this release: a TeamsRunner to deploy agents into Microsoft Teams, MultiRunConfig for running eval cases concurrently, and an adk generate_agent_card CLI command. There are breaking changes, for example enum tool arguments now arrive as enum members rather than strings.
google/elephant-goldfish-model
Python · Apache 2.0 · ~19.2k ★ (+5.5k this week)
A plugin Google's engineers used internally to catch AI coding mistakes. The "elephant" agent drafts a design with full context, then "goldfish" reviewers check it in fresh subagent sessions with no memory of how it was written, and you approve. It installs into Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity. Expect higher token use from all those review passes.
meta/astryx
TypeScript · MIT · ~28.2k ★ (+13.7k this week)
Meta's open-source React design system, built so coding agents can use it properly. It ships a CLI and an MCP server so agents can look up component docs and manifests instead of guessing props, plus ten built-in themes. It's still in beta, and it topped GitHub's weekly trending list this week.
ZhuLinsen/daily_stock_analysis
Python · ~72.3k ★ (+4.5k this week)
An LLM-powered stock analysis tool that covers several markets, pulls in news and runs on a schedule to produce daily reports. It's a good reference for the scheduled "fetch data, ask an LLM, publish a report" pattern, and it was on GitHub's weekly trending list this week.
Wild Card
An AI edited another AI's weights by hand, and its maths score jumped
In a blog post on 2 November, four MIT CSAIL researchers described "weight-space programming": they gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra the weights of Alibaba's small Qwen3.5-0.8B model, a Python session and some practice problems. Without any retraining, Astra changed about 0.016% of the weights and lifted the model's MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1%. The authors are clear that the edit mostly fixed a known looping bug rather than teaching the model maths, and on Google's Gemma 3 270M it barely helped (6.8% to 7.7% at best). Critics say the headlines oversold it. It's still a strange new way to debug a model: let another model open it up and poke around.

Personalised picks

Claude Data and Sheets are aimed at analyst work like yours. Claude Data, now generally available, and the Sheets beta are Anthropic going directly after the dashboards, spreadsheets and decks that your Analyst Agent already produces from Snowflake and dbt. If business teams at GXS can ask Claude Data for a chart themselves, the value of your work moves further toward governed metrics, trusted dbt models and judgement on what the numbers mean. It's worth a trial to see whether it could be the front end for your agent's outputs, or a competitor to them.

Fable 5.2 as an orchestrator changes your agents' costs. At $8/$40 per million tokens, Fable 5.2 is pricey to run every step of an agent. But Anthropic built it to act as an Advisor or Orchestrator that hands simpler tasks to smaller models. That fits your Analyst Agent and this digest agent well: let Fable plan the analysis or choose the stories, and let a cheaper model run the SQL, format the notebook or write the HTML. Fast mode is also worth testing for the interactive parts where stakeholders are waiting on an answer.

Better guardrails for the agentic products you're shipping with Grab. Google ADK 2.14 makes evaluation and sandboxed code execution stable, and its Python-first API fits your stack if any of the GXS × Grab agent work needs an open, model-agnostic framework with A2A between agents. Google's Elephant-Goldfish plugin is a cheap win for your own builds: its fresh-context reviewers would catch the kind of mistakes a coding agent makes when it marks its own work, which matters before anything touches customer data in a bank.
