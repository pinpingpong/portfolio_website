Chips Get Personal,

This week the frontier labs quietly started building their own silicon, infra money moved faster than model news, and Meta shipped something delightfully pointless.

Big News
Anthropic in talks with Samsung for custom AI chip

Anthropic is reportedly discussing a custom inference chip with Samsung, a move that looks like a direct response to OpenAI's newly announced in-house inference processor, "Jalapeño." Both labs are trying to cut their dependence on Nvidia and control the unit economics of serving their own models.

AnthropicChipsInfra
Qualcomm closes $3.9B acquisition of Modular

Qualcomm confirmed its acquisition of Modular, maker of the hardware-agnostic AI runtime MAX, which lets teams write an inference workload once and run it across Nvidia, AMD, or Snapdragon silicon. It's a bet that portability, not raw FLOPs, becomes the next competitive edge in inference.

M&AInference
Mistral's next open-weight model lands early access in July

Mistral confirmed a new open-weight model with early access opening this month. The French lab's ARR jumped from $20M to over $400M in a year and is on track to cross $1B in 2026, proof that open-weight releases can still be a serious commercial strategy.

Open WeightMistral
Baseten raises $1.5B Series F at a $13B valuation

Baseten, which sells the systems software teams use to actually run AI workloads in production, closed its fourth raise in 18 months. The size of the round says as much about where the money is flowing — deployment and inference infra, not just new foundation models — as any single model launch this week.

Wild Card
Meta quietly launches "Pocket," an app that turns text prompts into mini games

Buried under the week's infrastructure headlines, Meta shipped an experimental app called Pocket that generates playable, shareable mini games from a text prompt. No fanfare, no keynote — just a strange, fun side project from a company otherwise talking about turning spare AI compute into cash.

Pick of the Week
01
The custom silicon race just went from rumor to reality
Anthropic exploring a Samsung-built chip, on the heels of OpenAI's own "Jalapeño" inference processor, marks the moment the two biggest frontier labs both decided that renting Nvidia compute forever isn't the plan. Combined with the "token bill comes due" cost-overrun stories circulating this year, this is the clearest signal yet that 2026's AI economics fight is shifting from "who has the best model" to "who controls the cost per token" — a dynamic that will eventually filter down into what every downstream API costs to build on.
