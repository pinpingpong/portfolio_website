Issue #16 · 25 Oct 2026 · Weekly AI Digest

Claude Gets Proactive,
OpenAI Lands On Google,
Banks Push Back On Agents

Anthropic previews Claude Duet, OpenAI's models arrive on Google Cloud, OpenAI and DeepSeek chase giant new rounds, and US banks find unvetted AI agents inside customer accounts.

Big News
Anthropic previews Claude Duet, a proactive personal agent
On 21 October Anthropic opened a waitlist-only research preview of Claude Duet, its answer to Meta Muse and OpenAI Dots, for US Pro and Max subscribers on iOS, Android and claude.ai. Duet works in the background — morning briefs, drafting and preparing tasks — but Anthropic says it waits for confirmation before sending messages, paying, signing or deleting anything. There is no extra fee; usage, including its background checks, counts against existing plan limits. Team and Enterprise plans aren't included yet.

OpenAI's models come to Google Cloud
OpenAI and Google Cloud announced on 21 October that GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, plus Codex agentic workflows, are coming to Vertex AI Model Garden. They are in private preview for select enterprise customers now, with general availability promised "in the coming weeks" and OpenAI models later headed to Gemini for Business and Google Antigravity. Purchases can count against Google Cloud spending commitments, and coverage notes it is the first time OpenAI's models are sold natively on all three major US clouds.

Mega-rounds: OpenAI eyes $1.25T, DeepSeek reportedly raises $15B
CNBC reported on 22 October that OpenAI is working on a raise of up to $30B at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, led by Coatue and UAE investors — below the roughly $1.4 trillion floated a month earlier, and with terms still under negotiation. The same day Bloomberg reported that DeepSeek has raised about $15B (around 100 billion yuan), with Tencent and CATL among the reported backers, just two days after the US ordered federal agencies to remove DeepSeek's technology from their systems. Elsewhere, Tenstorrent closed a $1.5B Series E and planning platform Pigment raised $250M at a $2.3B valuation.
FundingMarkets
Unvetted AI agents are turning up in US bank accounts
Axios reported on 22 October that major US banks have found third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts without the bank's permission. Morgan Stanley has reportedly begun blocking third-party agents, while JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are restricting them, and some banks are moving toward read-only agent access. Axios documented no confirmed losses — the agents were largely following their users' instructions — but the story put agent authorisation squarely on regulators' radar.

Tools Worth Knowing
Agent Skills → Agentic AI Foundation
Open standard · 70+ platforms
On 22 October Anthropic donated the Agent Skills spec, name, GitHub org and agentskills.io to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, which already hosts MCP. Maintainers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and others will steward it; the SKILL.md format itself doesn't change for authors.
Gemini in Google Sheets for BigQuery
Google Workspace · 19 Oct
Connect a Sheet to BigQuery and ask Gemini questions in plain language; queries run against the full BigQuery dataset rather than just the rows visible in the spreadsheet. BigQuery also added CSV/PDF export for conversational analytics answers.
stablyai/orca
MIT · ~84k–104k ★ (sources vary)
An open-source environment for running a fleet of coding agents in parallel — Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and other CLI agents — each in its own git worktree, with your own subscriptions and a mobile companion app for checking status.
heygen-com/hyperframes
Apache-2.0 · trending this week
HeyGen's open-source "video as code" framework: agents write HTML/CSS compositions and the CLI renders them to MP4 via headless Chrome and FFmpeg. Installs as agent skills for Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI and Codex.
Wild Card
Claude writes ~180 million lines of proofs for 123 open-source libraries
Anthropic's Frontier Red Team and formal-methods startup Theorem turned Google OSS-Fuzz tests into formal specifications and had Claude prove that compiled code from 123 libraries never crashes on inputs under 64MB — roughly 180 million lines of Rocq proofs, checked by machine, produced over a few weeks. Along the way Claude found hundreds of bugs that had survived trillions of fuzzing runs. The caveats are real (single-threaded, statically linked RISC-V binaries only), but a subset of the proofs is public on GitHub and Theorem is releasing a Claude skill to help developers verify their own code.

For You — Pin Xiu
Personalised picks

Banks vs. unvetted agents. As you deploy more agentic products at GXS with Grab, this is the question your risk and compliance partners will ask next: which agents may act on a customer's account, and with what scope? The US banks' move toward read-only access is a useful pattern — default your customer-facing agents to read and recommend, and gate anything that moves money behind explicit confirmation, the same line Claude Duet draws.

Agent Skills goes neutral — and gets a malware problem. Your Analyst Agent, MapleStory Daily Agent and this very digest all run on SKILL.md SOPs, and the format now has multi-vendor governance, which makes your skills more portable. The same week, VirusTotal reported 6,637 malicious skills out of 35,878 analysed, including 90 that hide instructions in blank-line padding, HTML comments or invisible Unicode. Before teammates install your Analyst Agent with one bash script, read any third-party skills it pulls in as raw text, not rendered previews.

Gemini in Sheets on BigQuery. This is a mainstream version of what your Content Analytics Agent does — plain-English questions over a warehouse, answered with real queries. It's worth comparing against your guardrailed NL-to-SQL design on DuckDB: where Google lets the model hit the full dataset, your guardrails and auto-charts are the differentiators to highlight in interviews and case studies.

Pick of the Week
01
Agent permissions are the new battleground
In one week, banks started blocking unvetted agents, Anthropic shipped a proactive agent that pauses before acting, and VirusTotal showed how skills can smuggle hidden instructions past human review. Capability is no longer the bottleneck for agents — authorisation and trust are. Watch for bank and regulator guidance on agent access in the coming weeks, and design your own agents with scoped permissions and human confirmation from day one.
