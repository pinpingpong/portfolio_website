Anthropic and OpenAI ship flagships 90 minutes apart and slash prices, Meta's Muse agent climbs the app charts, and the enterprise conversation shifts from building agents to governing them.

Big News
Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol & Luna land on the same day — and both get cheaper
Anthropic released Opus 5.5 on 22 September as a more token-efficient model priced at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens — about 20% below Opus 5, and roughly 40% cheaper to run in practice. OpenAI answered within the hour with GPT-6 Sol (complex work like coding) and GPT-6 Luna (high-volume extraction and summarisation), halving API prices versus GPT-5.6. The frontier race is now as much about cost-per-task as benchmark scores.
ModelsPricing
Meta throws its weight behind Muse as the personal agent app takes off
Muse has passed 3.4 million downloads and is topping app store charts. Meta is promoting it across its own apps and announced a roadmap including video chat with the Muse avatar, computer use on the Mac, a dedicated email address for the agent, more connectors, and smart-glasses integration — a clear bid for the consumer "personal agent" slot.
AgentsConsumer
Google turns Antigravity into a managed agent harness in the Gemini API
A new antigravity-preview-09-2026 harness brings the Antigravity coding agent's tools and behaviour into AI Studio and the Interactions API on Gemini 3.8 Flash. New Files and Credentials APIs move data in and out of the agent sandbox and let agents call services like GitHub or Slack without exposing tokens to the model.
AgentsDeveloper Tools
Big cheques keep flowing: TEKEVER, Brahma AI, Noxtua
European autonomous-systems maker TEKEVER raised $580M at a $6.4B valuation, Brahma AI closed $150M at a $2B post-money valuation, and Berlin legal-AI company Noxtua raised more than €100M with publisher C.H.BECK becoming its majority shareholder. Money is concentrating in vertical, domain-heavy AI rather than generic model wrappers.

Wild Card
Anthropic's biology lab says it found a CRISPR-like enzyme system
Anthropic announced that its in-house biology lab has already made what it believes is a significant discovery: a new enzyme "system" with properties reminiscent of CRISPR. It's an early claim, but a notable one — an AI lab positioning itself as a producer of wet-lab science, not just a supplier of models to scientists.

Personalised picks

GPT-6 Luna and the new price floor. Your Analyst Agent at GXS runs extraction, analysis and deck creation — exactly the high-volume, summarise-and-extract workload Luna is pitched at. With both Anthropic and OpenAI cutting prices this week, it's a good moment to re-benchmark cost-per-run for your agent pipelines and see whether a cheap tier can handle routing and extraction while a frontier model does the reasoning. The same trick you used to cut Snowflake spend by 60% applies to token spend.

Dataiku Agent Management. As you roll out more agentic products to GXS with Grab, the questions shift from "does it work?" to "which agents exist, what KPIs do they move, and which are risky?" — the questions a regulated bank will be asked first. Dataiku's inventory-and-risk-tiering framing (with Databricks, Cortex and OpenTelemetry support) is a useful template for how to present agent governance to risk and compliance stakeholders, even if you never buy the tool.

Agent memory: hindsight, mem0, cognee. Your Content Analytics Agent already carries follow-up context within a chat, and your AI Twin uses Pinecone-backed RAG. Agent-memory libraries go a step further — remembering what worked across sessions. Try hindsight on a personal project: an analytics agent that remembers which SQL patterns and chart types a user prefers would be a strong, demo-able upgrade for your portfolio.

The frontier price war changes the agent maths
When two top labs cut prices on the same afternoon, the economics of agentic workflows shift overnight — multi-step agents that were too expensive to run on every request suddenly pencil out. Treat this week as a trigger to revisit which use cases you shelved on cost grounds, and to design agents with a model-routing layer so you can capture the next price cut without a rewrite.
