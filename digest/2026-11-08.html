Issue #18 · 08 Nov 2026 · Weekly AI Digest

AI Stocks Slide,
Fal Doubles Up,
Claude Reads Your Bank

AI stocks slid toward a correction after the US midterms and OpenAI promised to pay for its data centres' power, while Fal doubled its valuation to $16B, Salesforce agreed to buy Cresta, and Claude can now read your bank transactions.

Big News
AI stocks slide after the midterms, and OpenAI promises to pay its own power bills
On 5 November, the day after the US midterms, the AP reported the Nasdaq heading into correction territory as AI stocks fell. Reports put the index down about 4% on the day, with Nvidia down 5–8% and memory and chip makers hit harder. Commentary tied the drop to stretched valuations and to midterm results that showed voters pushing back on rapid data-centre buildouts. The same day, OpenAI pledged to pay the full electricity and grid-upgrade costs at data centres it owns and operates, to stop using NDAs with public officials, and to publish construction plans. The pledge isn't legally binding and gives no dollar figures.
MarketsOpenAIPolicy
Fal raises $1.3B and doubles its valuation to $16B
Bloomberg reported on 3 November that Fal, a San Francisco startup that runs image, video and audio models for developers, raised $1.3B in a round led by Bessemer Venture Partners. That puts it at $16B, double the $8B it was valued at in April. Nvidia, T. Rowe Price, Sound Ventures and HOF Capital reportedly also invested. Secondhand reports of the Bloomberg piece put Fal's annualised revenue at about $800M, roughly 20× revenue. Fal says the money will go on more compute and a bigger team.
FundingInference
Salesforce agrees to buy voice-AI company Cresta
Salesforce signed a definitive agreement on 2 November to acquire Cresta, whose voice AI handles contact-centre calls for customers including Marriott, Comcast and United Airlines. Salesforce didn't disclose terms; The Information reported a price of about $2.7B. Cresta will be built into Service Cloud so that every phone conversation links to the customer record. The deal is expected to close in Salesforce's first quarter of fiscal 2028, subject to regulatory approval, and it's the company's fifth customer-experience acquisition this year.
AcquisitionVoice AIAgents
Tencent ships Hy4 with vision and computer use
Tencent made its flagship Hy4 model generally available on 4 November, two months after the August preview, adding image understanding and the ability to operate a computer. It launched as API-only, with Tencent saying weights and a system card will follow. A cheaper, faster Hy4 Flash is planned next. The benchmark claims come from Tencent's own tests, and the launch didn't include standard coding benchmarks such as SWE-bench, so wait for independent results before comparing it with Western models.

Tools Worth Knowing
Perplexity Teammates
Perplexity Max · 5 Nov · web
Persistent, named agents built on Perplexity Computer that take on recurring work, watch the systems you assign them and act when something changes. Each one runs in its own environment with separate browser sessions, and they can work with each other and message coworkers in Slack. They draw on Max plan credits, and iOS is coming soon.
microsoft/apm
MIT · ~2–3k ★
Agent Package Manager: an npm-style dependency manager for AI agent setups. You declare the prompts, skills, instructions and MCP servers your agent needs in one manifest, and teammates install them with one command. Star counts vary between trackers, so check the repo for the live figure.
max-sixty/worktrunk
Rust · MIT · ~4.9k ★
A CLI that makes Git worktrees easy to use so you can run several coding agents in parallel, each on its own branch and folder, without them overwriting each other's changes. It replaces the long native git commands with short ones. It's from the creator of PRQL and was on GitHub's weekly trending list this week.
GitHub Copilot connectors
GitHub · public preview · all plans
GitHub-verified MCP servers that you sign in to once and then use across Copilot surfaces, including VS Code. 26 services are supported at launch, including Slack, Notion, Figma, Sentry, Linear, Atlassian Rovo and Vercel. Business and Enterprise orgs need the preview-features policy turned on. Announced 28 October.
Wild Card
An AI agent performed a brain injection on a live mouse
Wired reported on 30 October that San Francisco biotech Exin Therapeutics let an AI agent run a gene-therapy injection into the brain of a mouse with epilepsy. The agent chose one of 19 candidate therapies from a spreadsheet, picked the brain region and coordinates, then controlled the robotic arms and injector, including volume and speed. A veterinary surgeon did the craniotomy, and humans approved the plan and kept an emergency stop. Reports say one Claude model refused to guide the needle before another went ahead, though accounts differ on which. The mouse is doing well, and it isn't yet known whether the seizures dropped.

Personalised picks

Claude can now read people's bank accounts. Anthropic's Personal Finance beta, launched 5 November for US Pro, Max and Ultra users, links bank and card accounts through Plaid with read-only access, never sees passwords, and doesn't train on those chats. At GXS that's both competition and a blueprint: customers will soon expect to ask an assistant about their own spending, and the trust model (read-only, revocable, no training) is the bar your agentic products with Grab will be judged against. It's also a useful contrast for your churn work — an outside assistant that knows a customer's money better than the bank does weakens the bank's relationship with them.

A cleaner way to ship your Analyst Agent. Your Analyst Agent runs in VS Code Copilot and installs with one bash script. Copilot connectors now let teammates sign in once to tools like Slack, Notion and Atlassian Rovo from inside Copilot, so the agent could post findings or pull Confluence context without anyone managing tokens. Microsoft's APM goes further: a single manifest that pins the agent's prompts, skills and MCP servers, so every analyst gets the same version. Snowflake isn't on the connector list yet, so your governed .env setup stays for now.

Persistent agents and computer use are going mainstream. Perplexity Teammates gives each agent its own environment and a standing job, the same idea as your scheduled MapleStory Daily Agent and this digest agent, now sold as a product. Tencent's Hy4 adds computer use to another major model family. That gives you more options if you ever want a cheaper model for the repetitive clicking while keeping Claude for the judgment calls. The Exin mouse story is a reminder of why your hard guardrails and audit reports matter: humans approved every irreversible step.

Pick of the Week
01
Does the public selloff reach private AI valuations?
Public AI stocks fell into a correction in the same week Fal doubled its private valuation to about 20× revenue. One of these has to give. Watch whether late-stage rounds shrink or slow over the coming weeks, as OpenAI's already did, and how the market prices Anthropic's IPO. Also watch whether the political backlash on data centres turns into rules: OpenAI's power pledge is voluntary, but talk of a moratorium after the midterms means compute costs could rise. That would reach API prices, and through them the cost of every agent you run.
